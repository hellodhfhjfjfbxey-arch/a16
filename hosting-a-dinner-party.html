Guide I

Hosting a Dinner Party

The best dinner parties feel effortless because the work was done earlier. Here is how to plan one so you can enjoy it too.

Course I

Choose your guests and a date

Six to eight people is a comfortable number for one conversation around one table. Invite two to three weeks ahead, mention the time and anything guests should know, and ask about allergies and diets when they reply, not on the night.

Course II

Plan a menu you can make ahead

Choose dishes that wait happily for you.

  • A cold or room-temperature starter.
  • A main that braises or roasts while you sit with guests.
  • A dessert finished the day before.

Cook only one dish you have not made before, and try it once first if you can.

Course III

Make a timeline

Work backwards from the time you will sit down. Shop two days before, prepare sauces and desserts the day before, and set the table that morning. Write the schedule down and stick it inside a cupboard door so you never have to think on the night.

Course IV

Set the scene

Lower the main lights and add candles and a few flowers kept low enough to see over. Choose quiet music that will not compete with conversation. Clear a space for coats and bags, and have water and something to nibble ready as guests arrive.

Course V

Enjoy the evening

Seat yourself near the kitchen so you can slip away easily. Serve family style to save time, let guests help clear if they offer, and leave the washing up until later. The guests remember the company far more than any small mistake in the kitchen.
