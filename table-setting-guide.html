Guide II

How to Set a Dinner Table

A well-set table tells guests they are welcome. The rules are simpler than they look: everything sits in the order it will be used.

Course I

Start with the plate

Place the dinner plate in the centre of each setting, about two centimetres from the table edge. Allow around 60 cm of table width per person so elbows have room. A charger or place mat underneath frames each setting and protects the table.

Course II

Cutlery, outside in

Cutlery is laid in the order it will be used, starting from the outside.

  • Forks on the left of the plate.
  • Knives on the right, blades facing the plate, then a soup spoon if needed.
  • Dessert spoon and fork above the plate, handles pointing to the side they will be picked up from.

Course III

Glasses and side plate

The water glass sits above the knife. Any other glasses go to its right, in the order they will be used. The side plate for bread sits above the forks, with a small butter knife resting across it.

Course IV

Napkins

Fold napkins simply and place them on the plate or to the left of the forks. Cloth napkins feel generous and can be reused for years. A ribbon, a sprig of herbs or a name card tucked into the fold adds a personal touch without fuss.

Course V

Flowers and candles

Keep centrepieces low, below eye level, so guests can see each other across the table. Use unscented candles near food so they do not compete with the dinner, and leave room in the middle for serving dishes if you are eating family style.
